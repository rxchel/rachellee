# Fundamental Study of Germanium Nanowire Growth and Deposition

**Electrochemistry · Nanomaterials · Semiconductor Materials · Data Analysis · Analytical Chemistry**

## Project Overview

This project investigated the **electrochemical liquid–liquid–solid (ec-LLS) crystal growth of germanium (Ge)** nanowires to better understand the fundamental mechanisms governing their growth and deposition.

Ge nanowires are of interest for applications in **battery anodes, nanoelectronics, and optoelectronic devices**. The ec-LLS growth mechanism provides a lower-energy and lower-resource approach to semiconductor crystal synthesis compared with traditional methods.

The goal of this work was to study the formation of individual Ge nanowires on **ultramicroelectrodes (UMEs)** and investigate how Ge deposits on different electrode materials. Developing a better understanding of Ge crystal growth can ultimately help control nanowire characteristics such as **length, thickness, and morphology**, which are important for scaling nanowire-based materials toward energy-storage applications.

## My Contributions

I contributed to the experimental development, fabrication, characterization, and analysis of the electrochemical system used to study Ge nanowire growth.

### Ultramicroelectrode Fabrication

I developed and refined procedures for fabricating **micropipettes, nanopipettes, microelectrodes, and ultramicroelectrodes (UMEs)** for electrochemical experiments.

I fabricated platinum and copper UMEs by inserting metal wires into glass capillaries and using micropipette pulling equipment to produce electrodes with micrometer-scale dimensions. I worked with both the P-97 Flaming/Brown Micropipette Puller and P-2000 Laser-Based Micropipette Puller and optimized fabrication procedures to improve experimental efficiency.

### Electrochemical Characterization

I characterized fabricated UMEs using **cyclic voltammetry** to evaluate their electrochemical behavior and determine their effective dimensions.

I collected cyclic voltammograms using a three-electrode cell and analyzed the resulting data with OriginPro. I fitted the voltammetric response to determine the steady-state current and calculate UME dimensions, obtaining an average electrode radius of approximately 16.7 μm.

### Germanium Nanowire Growth

I studied Ge deposition using the ec-LLS crystal growth mechanism, in which Ge ions are reduced from solution, dissolve into a eutectic gallium-indium (e-GaIn) droplet, and subsequently form crystalline Ge once the liquid phase becomes supersaturated.

I investigated Ge growth on Pt and Cu ultramicroelectrodes and examined the resulting deposits using optical and electron microscopy. I analyzed differences in Ge deposition between the two electrode materials to better understand the factors influencing nanowire growth.

### Experimental Analysis

I analyzed microscopy and electrochemical data to investigate where Ge was deposited and how the electrode and experimental conditions affected the resulting structures.

The experiments showed a significant presence of Ge on approximately half of the Pt UMEs analyzed, while the Cu UMEs examined did not show significant Ge deposition. I also observed changes in the e-GaIn droplet after applying bias, providing insight into the electrochemical processes occurring during growth.

## Results

The project demonstrated the feasibility of studying individual Ge growth sites using electrochemical methods and micrometer-scale electrodes.

I fabricated and characterized Pt and Cu UMEs and investigated their behavior during Ge deposition. The resulting microscopy data showed differences in Ge deposition between the two electrode materials, with significant Ge observed on approximately half of the Pt UMEs analyzed and no significant Ge observed on the Cu UMEs examined.

These observations provided insight into the factors affecting Ge deposition and established a foundation for further investigation of controlled Ge nanowire growth.
