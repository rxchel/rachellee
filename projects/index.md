### Ab initio Electron-Molecule Scattering Calculations for Methane Using UKRMol+ and ASTRA

**Dr. Thomas C. Allison and Dr. Siddhartha Chattopadhyay · National Institute of Standards and Technology**

Computational investigation of low-energy electron scattering from methane 
using UKRmol+ and ASTRA. The project explores scattering cross sections,
Ramsauer-Townsend minima, and resonance features for plasma modeling 
relevant to semiconductor manufacturing and chip etching.

**Tools:** UKRMol+, ASTRA, Psi4, Python, Linux

### Open-Source 3D-Printed Demonstrations for Visualizing Transverse Wave Motion

**Dr. Abhishek Sharma · The Cooper Union**

I designed and prototyped 3D-printed mechanical demonstrations that visualize transverse wave motion through physical oscillation, with the goal of creating accessible and engaging tools for science education.

I developed and iterated multiple designs using OnShape and Autodesk Fusion 360, experimenting with different axis, spoke, and support geometries to improve the visibility, stability, flexibility, and printability of the demonstrations. I also fabricated prototypes using PLA and evaluated their mechanical behavior and performance through successive design iterations.

The project explored how low-cost, open-source 3D-printed models can provide tactile and visual approaches to science education. The designs were developed with the goal of making the demonstrations freely available and adaptable for broader educational use.

**Tools:** CAD, OnShape, Autodesk Fusino 360, 3D Printing, Prototyping, Science Communication

[View the project presentation (PDF)](https://rxchel.github.io/rachellee/assets/files/transverse-waves/presentation.pdf)

### Comparing the Energy Requirements for Maple Syrup Production through Boiling and Freezing Methods

**The Cooper Union**

I investigated the energy efficiency of boiling and freezing methods for maple syrup production, comparing traditional evaporation with an alternative freezing-based concentration process.

I experimentally produced syrup using both methods and analyzed the heat and energy requirements of each process using thermodynamic principles, including specific heat capacities, phase changes, enthalpy, and refrigeration-cycle analysis. The freezing method required substantially less energy than boiling, although it produced a less concentrated syrup under the experimental conditions.

This project demonstrated how thermodynamic analysis and process comparison can be used to evaluate energy-efficient alternatives to conventional chemical processes, with broader relevance to sustainable process design and energy conservation.

**Tools:** Thermodynamics, Energy Analysis, Experimental Design, Data Analysis
